# Research Brief

> Use for `technical-research`. Sections are questions, not sources; the reader should be able to stop
> after the first section and still make the decision.

```markdown
# Research: <question as asked>

## Answer

<2–5 sentences. Direct. Include the confidence level. State the boundary: valid for <versions/scope>.>

## Scope

- Questions: Q1 ..., Q2 ...
- Versions / time window: ...
- Explicitly out of scope: ...

## Findings

### Q1 — <question>
- **Established:** <claim> [VERIFIED|LIKELY] — <source, version, date>
- **Contested:** <claim> — <source A> vs <source B>; difference explained by <...>
- **Unknown:** <gap and what would resolve it>

### Q2 — ...

## Contradicting evidence

| Source | Claims | Conflicts with | Relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Source ledger

| # | Source | Version/date | Accessed | Tier | What it actually says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Limits

- Access limits: <paywall, no network, unavailable docs>
- Non-reproducible claims: <...>
- Saturation: <when searching stopped producing new sources>

## What would change this answer

- <observation> would move the conclusion from <A> to <B>.
```

## Discipline

- Every row in the ledger must be a source you opened. A search-result snippet is not a source.
- "Established" requires agreement across independent primaries or one normative source.
- If the honest answer is "not established", write that in the Answer section.
